Books children can actually read. Assessment that teaches. A multigrade classroom that works.

Semillas de lectura is a proposal to teach reading in Spanish in rural schools where a single teacher teaches several grades at once.

See the approach Browse the collection

The problem

One classroom, very different reading histories

In a rural multigrade school, children with very different reading journeys share a single classroom. The materials available were not designed for that reality.

  • There are no books ordered by difficulty in Spanish for that reality. Either sequences built for other languages are imported, or texts are «simplified» without criteria.
  • Assessment labels instead of guiding. It often assigns a «level» rather than telling the teacher what to teach next.
  • Equity arrives late. Those who come with another language or another way of speaking are treated as a special case, not as the starting point of the design.

The approach

Three pieces that work together

None works alone: the books state what they demand, the short test becomes teaching, and the classroom material organizes the group around that priority.

1

Books that state their difficulty

Every book says which letters and letter combinations its texts use, in four grades of difficulty, and describes its language: vocabulary, syntax, discourse, knowledge and image. This lets the teacher choose a book by what the text demands, without putting a fixed label on the child.

2

A short test that guides teaching

A short task test shows what to focus on (letters and combinations, fluency, language and comprehension, or reading more) and what evidence there is. It gives no scores or levels: it can conclude that it is still too early to decide.

3

Multigrade classroom material

Temporary groups by objective, tasks the child can do alone, flexible re-entry for those who missed class, classroom routines and teacher support. The group is organized by what needs to be taught, not by fixed labels.

What makes it different

Five design decisions

These are decisions, not promises: they define how books are written, how learning is measured, and how teaching is organized.

  • No fixed label is put on the child. The teaching priority is mobile and revisable: it organizes temporary groups and changes with how the child responds.
  • Variety is not error. Andean Spanish is accepted as a legitimate way of speaking; only what changes the word is corrected. Without these rules, a test would read a child's way of speaking as an error.
  • Culture is not decoration. Local and Andean content goes through community consultation and authorization before publication; values are shown in the story, not as a moral.
  • Build once, verify light. The sequence, the vocabulary, the books, the tests and the classroom material are built once for the whole country; each territory only checks that they work.
  • Honesty about status. Every decision, hypothesis and open issue is registered and marked as such: no hypothesis is presented as a result.

Status and transparency

The design is complete, not yet tested in the field

Honesty about status is part of the method. Stating precisely what is complete and what is not protects the project and its funders: a complete design does not turn a hypothesis into a tested result.

What is complete

  • Seven technical documents with the evidence, the design and how it will be validated.
  • The 20-chapter book that explains the complete design.
  • 28 print-ready books and a world guide with teaching sheets.
  • The v1 reading test with its teacher sheet and the map of 100 titles.
Not yet tested in the field.

The printed editions are first working editions. Before any data on minors is collected, the project must close:

  • Expert and community review of the base material and the local texts.
  • Testing the titles with real readers.
  • Closing and authorizing the ethics and data plan.
  • Agreements with local and regional education authorities, and coordination with bilingual intercultural education.

The road to the first application in the field

Six steps to the first application in the field

The goal is not to measure impact. The first application in the field will study whether the proposal is viable, how it is applied, what obstacles appear and what it costs, in order to adjust the material before scaling it.

  1. Base material

    The sequence of letters and combinations, the Andean children's vocabulary, the variety rules, the rules for making decisions and the ethics and data plan. Nothing can start without this.

  2. Authors and illustrators

    Early commissions and delivery: writing, illustrating and getting cultural authorization is what takes longest.

  3. Tests

    The reading test, the teacher sheet and its texts, in parallel with the books.

  4. Tested titles

    Between 6 and 10 contrast titles, reviewed by two teams separately and tested with readers.

  5. Classroom material

    Assembled last, from books and tests already tried, and verified in a multigrade classroom.

  6. First application in the field

    The hard test in a demanding territory. If it works there, it can be applied elsewhere.
