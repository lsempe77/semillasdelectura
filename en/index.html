Books children can actually read. Assessment that teaches. A multigrade classroom that works.

A national proposal for early literacy in Spanish for Peru's rural multigrade schools. First entry: Apurímac, Antabamba district. Equity for Quechua L1 readers by design.

See the approach Browse the collection

The problem

One classroom, very different reading histories

In a rural multigrade school, children with very different reading journeys share a single classroom, and in the Andes many are early Quechua speakers. The materials available were not designed for that reality.

  • There are no leveled Spanish texts for that reality. Either progressions built for English are imported, or texts are «simplified» without criteria.
  • Assessment labels instead of guiding. It often assigns a «level» rather than telling the teacher what to teach next.
  • Equity arrives late. The Quechua L1 reader is treated as a special case, not as the starting point of the design.

The approach

Three pieces that work together

None works alone: the books state their demand, screening turns into teaching, and the kit organizes the classroom around that priority.

1

Books designed for a purpose

Every text states its code demand (C1–C4, against an explicit sequence of Spanish) and its language profile (vocabulary, syntax, discourse, knowledge, image). Two descriptions, never a single band that labels the child.

2

Screening that becomes teaching

A short task route yields a teaching priority (code, fluency, language-comprehension, extension) and a state of the evidence. No scores, no levels: deciding nothing is a legitimate outcome.

3

Multigrade classroom kit

Temporary groups by objective, certified independent tasks, re-entry for those who missed class, functional routines and teacher support. Priority organizes mobile groups, not fixed identities.

What makes it different

Five design decisions

These are decisions, not promises: they define how books are written, how learning is measured, and how teaching is organized.

  • No child has a level. Priority is mobile and revisable: it organizes temporary teaching groups and changes with the response to teaching.
  • Variety is not error. Andean Spanish is accepted as a legitimate variety; only what changes the word is corrected. Without these rules, an instrument would read a Quechua L1 child's pronunciation as deficit.
  • Culture is not decoration. Situated content goes through community consultation and authorization before publication; values are embodied in the story, never as a moral.
  • Build once, verify light. Scale, corpus, books, instruments and kit are national assets that are not rebuilt per district; each site runs a light, standardized verification.
  • Honesty about status. Decisions, hypotheses and open issues are registered and kept separate: no hypothesis is presented as a result.

Status and transparency

Architecture complete, validation pending

Honesty about status is part of the method. A complete documentation architecture does not turn a hypothesis into a validated result.

What is complete

  • Documentation architecture PT1–PT7 with evidence, design and validation strategy.
  • A 20-chapter manuscript explaining the complete design.
  • 18 print-ready books produced and a world guide with teaching sheets.
  • A v1 screening instrument with a teacher sheet and a map of 100 titles.
Not yet validated in the field.

The printed editions are first working editions. Before any data on minors is collected, the project must close:

  • Expert and community review of the national assets and situated texts.
  • Testing of editorial prototypes with real readers.
  • A closed and authorized ethics and data plan.
  • Territorial agreements (UGEL/DRE) and EIB coordination.

The road to the first entry

Six steps to Antabamba

The goal is not an impact pilot. It is a feasibility, process, exposure, cost and adverse signal study: how much local adjustment the national core needs, so it can be standardized.

  1. National assets

    Reference sequence for code, Andean children's corpus, variety rules, governance and the ethics and data plan. They unblock everything.

  2. Authors and illustrators

    Briefs and early handoff: writing, illustrating and cultural authorization are the critical path.

  3. Instruments

    Screening, teacher sheet and leveled stimuli, in parallel with the books.

  4. Tested prototypes

    6–10 contrast titles with independent double leveling and testing with real readers.

  5. Classroom kit

    Assembled last, from tested books and instruments: integrated and verified in a multigrade classroom.

  6. First entry

    Antabamba, a strongly Quechua L1 territory: the hard test of the light protocol. If it works there, it travels.
